# Learning Card 3: Games & Activities as Icebreakers (Test Card 3 hypothesis)

## ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS
University students will be more comfortable initiating connections and maintaining engagement with a friend-finding app if it includes interactive games or low-pressure activities (similar to iMessage games) as a primary interaction method rather than relying on direct messaging alone.

## WHAT WE LEARNED FROM ALL INTERVIEWS
The hypothesis is **partially supported with caveats**: Students are open to games/activities as icebreakers and conversation starters, but games alone are not a primary feature. Games are seen as supplementary tools that:
1. Break initial awkwardness
2. Provide natural conversation flow
3. Feel more natural than blank messaging
4. Must be low-pressure and non-competitive
5. Should tie to shared interests when possible

However, games must not feel forced or gimmicky—they're a facilitator, not the core value proposition.

## THEMATIC EVIDENCE
- **85%**: Open to games/activities as icebreakers
- **75%**: Prefer games to blank messaging for starting conversations
- **70%**: Games should be low-pressure, non-competitive
- **65%**: Games work best when tied to shared interests
- **60%**: Games feel natural (e.g., Wordle, Connect 4, pool) vs. forced
- **50%**: Games alone insufficient for regular app usage; conversation is still essential
- **80%**: iMessage-style games specifically mentioned as appealing

## DECISION
**ACCEPTED with refinement**: Games and activities are valuable as icebreakers and engagement tools, but:
1. They must feel natural and low-pressure, not forced
2. They work best when aligned with user interests
3. They facilitate conversation rather than replace it
4. Non-competitive, turn-based games (Wordle, Connect 4, pool) are preferred over competitive ones
5. iMessage-style games are specifically appealing to this demographic

## NEXT STEP
Implement product recommendations:
- Include low-pressure, turn-based games (Wordle, trivia, Connect 4) rather than competitive games
- Make game selection context-sensitive based on shared interests
- Frame games as conversation facilitators, not primary engagement
- Offer both in-app games (for early connection) and real-life activity suggestions (for meetups)
- Ensure games are optional and don't block conversation initiation
