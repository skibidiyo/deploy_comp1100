# Learning Card 1: Swipe-Based Friend Matching (Test Card 1 hypothesis)

## ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS
University students would be comfortable using a swipe-based mechanic — similar to Tinder — to discover and match with potential friends on campus.

## WHAT WE LEARNED FROM ALL INTERVIEWS
Across 23 interviews, students are broadly open to swipe-based friend matching — but with conditions. Acceptance rises sharply when profiles show interests and hobbies, not just photos. Several participants who initially hesitated (Hannah, Serafina #2, #3) shifted to positive once interests were added to profiles. One participant (Serafina #5) challenged mutual matching, preferring a one-sided swipe-to-connect model. Students consistently separate the concept from dating apps, and verification (UQ credentials) recurs as a trust and comfort factor.

## THEMATIC EVIDENCE
- ~75%: Open to the swipe mechanic for friend-finding (positive or conditionally positive)
- ~80%: Accept swiping when profiles include interests and hobbies, not just photos
- ~30%: Explicitly uncomfortable with photo-only swiping — "judging by looks"
- ~50%: Draw a clear distinction between friend-finding and dating apps
- ~35%: Mention safety/verification (UQ email, real identity) as a key comfort factor

## EVIDENCE FROM ALL STUDENTS BY SOURCE

### Anindiyo's Interviews
- **Anindiyo #1**: Past experience with Hoop app — "You're basically selecting who you're being friends with, and you're in control." (positive)
- **Anindiyo #2**: "I think it could work… if they could implement it for dating, I'm pretty sure they can make it work for finding friends." (positive)
- **Anindiyo #3**: "I feel like it's a good idea. It depends on what kind of things they actually show." (conditional)
- **Anindiyo #4**: "Kind of in the middle… it feels like you can't really feel the vibe of the other person… like a shopping catalog." (hesitant)
- **Anindiyo #5**: "I think it is a unique idea… fast. You can pick a friend easily based on the initial idea of themselves." (positive)

### Anya's Interviews

### Kai's Interviews

- **Kai #3**: Already uses Tinder with friend-finding intent — "I always go into the intention of just making friends, at least." (positive)
- **Kai #4**: Mixed dating app experience; didn't address friend-finding swipe specifically — interests are "pretty important." (neutral)
- **Kai #5**: "Mixed bag. Sometimes good, sometimes talks just die off." Didn't address friend-finding swipe. (neutral)

### Serafina's Interviews
- **Serafina #1**: "Yeah, it's actually very cool… I know people have preferences… it would be better if I know if we have the same interests before." (positive with interests caveat)
- **Serafina #2**: "I feel like it's like judging people too fast, just from photos." With interests added: "Oh I see, I think it's good." (hesitant → positive)
- **Serafina #3**: "Could be fun… simple and quick. But I feel strange because I'm judging people based on their picture." With more info: "I would feel better if I have more information, like their interests or hobbies." (hesitant → positive)
- **Serafina #4**: "Sounds fun… a very direct way of making friends. And easier." (positive)
- **Serafina #5**: Okay with the concept but dislikes mutual matching — "If one person can swipe right and maybe make a good connection, then why not?" Prefers one-sided connection model. (mixed)

### Shenal's Interviews
- **Shenal #1**: "Probably not [personally]… in an online scenario, common interests would help." (personal pass, sees value)
- **Shenal #2**: "Actually, I think that's pretty good… Bumble has a BFF mode… very important, especially for new students." (positive)
- **Shenal #3**: "That's a great idea, but it might be hard to get people on it… some people are still unsure about apps like Tinder. But overall, it's a good idea." (cautiously positive)

## DECISION
**CONDITIONALLY ACCEPTED**: The swipe mechanic is broadly acceptable, but only when profiles show interests and hobbies alongside photos. Photo-only swiping is rejected by a significant minority. App must be clearly positioned as friend-finding, not dating. Note: one participant challenged mutual matching — a one-sided connection model may reduce friction.

## NEXT STEP
- Show interests, hobbies, and study field prominently on swipe cards — not just photos.
- Require UQ email verification for all users.
- Implement mutual matching as default, but consider exploring a one-sided connection option.
- Clearly distinguish from dating apps in UI and onboarding.
