**HYPOTHESIS** 
Students are more likely to initiate and feel comfortable forming new connections when they know another person’s interests or hobbies beforehand.

**TEST**

To test this hypothesis, we will interview 25 current The University of Queensland students about their preferences when deciding whether to connect with someone new, both in-person and through digital platforms.

**METRICS**

Percentage of users who prefer knowing interests before connecting
Reported comfort level when interests are known vs unknown (1–5 scale)
Likelihood of initiating conversation when shared interests are visible
Time taken to initiate interaction
Qualitative feedback on why interests matter (or don’t)


**CRITERIA** 
We can accept the hypothesis if at least 70% of users indicate that knowing someone’s interests significantly increases their willingness to connect, and if the majority report higher comfort levels when shared interests are visible.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**

How important is it for you to know someone’s interests before connecting with them?
Would you be more likely to start a conversation if you shared a common interest? Why or why not?
What types of interests or information would you want to see before deciding to connect?
Have you ever connected with someone because of a shared interest? What happened?
