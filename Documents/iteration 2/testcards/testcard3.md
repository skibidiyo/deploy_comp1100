## HYPOTHESIS 6 - Games & Activities as Icebreakers

**HYPOTHESIS**
University students will be more comfortable initiating connections and maintaining engagement with a friend-finding app if it includes interactive games or low-pressure activities (similar to iMessage games) as a primary interaction method rather than relying on direct messaging alone.

**TEST**
To test whether games and structured activities reduce friction in initial connections, we will interview university students about their receptiveness to in-app games/activities, preferred game types, and how this compares to traditional swiping and direct messaging approaches.

**METRICS**
- % of users who express openness to games/activities as an icebreaker
- % who find games more comfortable than starting a blank conversation
- Types of games/activities mentioned as most appealing (word games, trivia, quick challenges, etc.)
- Comfort level (1–5 scale) when initiating via game vs. direct message
- % who cite games as a feature that would drive regular app usage
- Correlation between game receptiveness and overall app adoption likelihood
- Qualitative feedback on why games feel natural vs. forced
- Feedback on how games compare to existing platforms (Instagram, Discord)

**CRITERIA**
We can accept this hypothesis if:
- At least 70% express openness or interest in games/activities as an icebreaker
- At least 65% report that having "something to talk about" (shared prompt/game) reduces anxiety vs. blank messaging
- At least 2-3 consistent game/activity types emerge as universally appealing across interviews
- Average comfort level for game-based interaction is ≥4/5 or notably higher than direct messaging
- At least 60% cite interactive games as a feature that would encourage regular usage
- Qualitative responses indicate games feel "natural" or "low-pressure" rather than gimmicky
- At least 75% see games as a meaningful differentiator from existing platforms

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1. Have you heard of or used apps like Tinder or Bumble?
2. How do you feel about the idea of swiping on people to make friends?
3. How important is it to you to know someone's interests or hobbies before deciding to connect with them?
4. Do their interests need to be very specific and closely matched to yours, or is having generally similar interests enough?
5. What kind of things on a profile would make you feel comfortable enough to start a conversation?
6. What kind of group size feels most comfortable to you when meeting new people?
7. Do you find it helpful when there's something to talk about already — like a shared topic or a prompt — rather than starting from scratch?
8. Would you be open to a fun activity or game as a way to get to know someone new? What kind of activity would feel natural? (like an iMessage game thing)
9. Would knowing about nearby or upcoming events at campus related to your interests help you meet people in real life? How so?
10. What features would make you actually want to use a friendship app regularly?
11. What would make this app better than just using Instagram, Discord, or existing group chats?
