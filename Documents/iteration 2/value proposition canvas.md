Product: A tinder-like friend finding app for UQ students <br>
Customer segment: UQ students, specifically introverted / first year students <br>
Data source: 23 student interviews conducted on campus. <br>


## Iteration 1 Hypothesis Results

### ✅ Accepted

| # | Hypothesis | Result |
|---|-----------|--------|
| 1 | University students struggle to make friends due to social barriers and lack of context. | **Accepted** — came up across all interviews. Approach anxiety, lecture environments, and not knowing how to start were universal. Gets easier with shared tasks or events. |
| 2 | Students are more likely to form friendships when they share common interests or activities. | **Accepted** — interest was the "door opener" in almost every story. Students asked for interest visibility on profiles explicitly. |
| 3 | Most students prefer initial connections in group settings rather than one-on-one. | **Accepted** — groups reduce pressure at the start. One-on-one still valued, but only after some trust is already there. |
| 4 | Most students feel uncomfortable approaching new people due to social anxiety or low confidence. | **Accepted** — not really about confidence though, more about not having context. Anxiety drops when there's a shared reason to talk. |
| 5 | Students prefer a friend-finding app with interest-based matching and low-pressure features over direct 1-to-1 matching. | **Accepted** — strong preference for group chats, passive browsing, and shared interest prompts. Direct messaging feels too high-stakes upfront. |

### ❌ Refuted

*None in Iteration 1.*

## Iteration 2 Hypothesis Results

### ✅ Accepted

| # | Hypothesis | Result |
|---|-----------|--------|
| 4 | Showing interests/hobbies on profiles helps students feel comfortable reaching out. | **Accepted** — came up in basically every interview. Students said they don't know how to start a conversation without something in common. |
| 5 | Knowing about campus events related to your interests helps you meet people in real life. | **Accepted** — really well received. A lot of students said events are already how they've made most of their uni friends. |
| 2 | Students prefer meeting new people in small groups (2–5) over one-on-one or large groups. | **Accepted** — 3–5 people came up consistently. One-on-one felt too intense, large groups felt hard to break into. |
| 3 | Having games or activities in the app makes it easier to start talking to someone. | **Accepted** — most were open to it as an icebreaker, especially casual turn-based games. Not a replacement for chatting though. |

### ⚠️ Conditionally Accepted

| # | Hypothesis | Result |
|---|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Students would be okay using a swipe mechanic (like Tinder) to find friends. | **Conditionally Accepted** — fine if profiles show interests, not just photos. A few people felt photo-only swiping was too shallow. One person also didn't like the mutual match requirement. Needs to feel clearly different from a dating app. |

### ❌ Refuted

*None in Iteration 2.*
