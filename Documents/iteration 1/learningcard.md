## HYPOTHESIS
**We believed that**  
Making friends could be challenging for a lot of university students due to emotional barriers, lack of connection strategies, and limited opportunities to meet like-minded people.

---

## OBSERVATION
**We observed**  
- **Emotional barriers**: Most students struggle with overthinking, social anxiety, fear of judgment, and introversion when approaching new people
- **Key barriers to approaching**: Anxiety, People already in groups, Perceived busyness, Shyness, and Introversion
- **What works**: Shared context (same classes, clubs with shared interests, predetermined activities) significantly reduces friction to making friends
- **Preferences**: Majority prefer one-on-one or small group settings over large events
- **Current pain points**: 
  - Lack of structured introduction opportunities at events
  - Difficulty maintaining friendships formed at one-off events
  - Limited visibility of clubs and events that match interests
  - Existing apps not designed for university peer networks
  - Time and scheduling conflicts prevent club participation
- **Common themes*: All students expressed interest in better ways to make friends and many mentioned apps with interest-based matching

---

## LEARNING AND INSIGHTS
**From that we learnt that**  
1. **Shared context is the primary friendship catalyst** — Students successfully make friends through: same classes, clubs with specific interests, shared activities. The need for a "conversation starter" or commonality is universal.

2. **Low-pressure, structured settings beat open events** — Speed friending and club activities work better when they have clear structures or produce natural groupings. Unstructured large events leave people standing with their own friend groups.

3. **Interest alignment is non-negotiable** — Nearly every student mentioned needing to know someone's interests, hobbies, or course before connecting. A system that surfaces these would remove significant friction.

4. **Introversion and shyness are pervasive** — students identified as introverted; most experience social anxiety or overthinking. This segment is largely underserved by traditional club/event approaches.

5. **Platform discoverability is broken** — Multiple students explicitly didn't know friendship apps existed beyond Instagram. Even when apps exist (Discord, LINE), they're not designed for university socializing.

6. **The "one-off connection" problem** — Friendships formed at single events often don't persist. Students need ongoing context or reason to reconnect (classes, clubs, repeated activities).
