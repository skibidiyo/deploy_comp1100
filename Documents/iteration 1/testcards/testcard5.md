## HYPOTHESIS 5 - App Features

**HYPOTHESIS**
Students prefer a friend-finding app that includes interest-based matching and low-pressure interaction features (e.g., group chats or browsing profiles) over direct 1-to-1 matching.

**TEST**
To test the proposed hypotheses, we will interview 25 current The University of Queensland students about their experiences in forming peer networks on campus.

**METRICS**
Users who choose interest-based matches over random/default suggestions
Users who choose low-pressure interactions (group chat, profile browsing) vs direct messaging
Time taken to initiate first interaction
Self-reported comfort level (1–5 scale)
Qualitative feedback (hesitations, preferences, concerns)

**CRITERIA**
We can accept the hypothesis if at least 70% of users prefer interest-based matches and at least 60% of users choose low-pressure interaction methods first, and also if the majority express reduced anxiety compared to typical “friending” apps.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1. Have you used any apps, group chats, or platforms to connect with people? What did you dislike about the app?
2. If you could design an app to help you make friends, what features would you want it to have and why?
3. What would make you feel comfortable reaching out to someone new through an app?
