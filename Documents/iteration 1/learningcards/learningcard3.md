# Learning Card 3: Group interaction preference (Test Card 3 hypothesis)

## ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS
Most university students prefer initial connections in group settings rather than one-on-one.

## WHAT WE LEARNED FROM ALL INTERVIEWS
Strong consensus for groups as an initial stage; one-on-one is still valued later, but groups reduce pressure and pain of initiation.

## THEMATIC EVIDENCE
- group events, club meetings, tutorials are frequent friendship sources
- small to medium group size is preferred (large is intimidating, one-on-one can be intense)
- group settings provide safety, with others carrying parts of conversations
- some introverts still prefer one-on-one for depth, but after trust is built

## EVIDENCE FROM ALL STUDENTS BY SOURCE
- **Kai4**: "one-on-one can be pretty intense... group takes pressure off." 
- **Kai5**: "groups let you bounce off each other... more opportunities to meet someone like you."
- **Anindiyo2**: "smaller group; big group intimidating, but small group okay."
- **Serafina1**: group assignments are easiest because they give reason to speak; 1:1 more personal.
- **Shenal #2 (Nadeesha)**: prefers small groups; says one-on-one intense and large group overwhelming.
- **Anya (Haruka)**: debriefs that speed friending with structured group rotations helped but needs smaller/focused groups.
- **Anya (Lachlan)**: shows group settings more comfortable, but gets engaged via interest-flagged individuals inside groups.
- **Anya (Sora)**: prefers one-on-one, but says groups are a good way to start and reduce pressure.
- **Anya (Thomas)**: recommends group settings first then one-on-one after comfort is built.

## DECISION
**ACCEPTED (refined)**. Group-first onboarding is validated; preserve project path to 1:1 conversions.

## NEXT STEP
- Build a small-group matching feature that randomly places users into small groups based on shared interests and preferred group size.
- Enable simple transition from group chat to private 1:1 chat after connection.
