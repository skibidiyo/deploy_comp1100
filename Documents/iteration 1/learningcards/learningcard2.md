# Learning Card 2: Shared interests foster friendships (Test Card 2 hypothesis)

## ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS
University students are more likely to form friendships when they share common interests or activities.

## WHAT WE LEARNED FROM ALL INTERVIEWS
Confirmed: shared interest is the “door opener” used by nearly every participant. Students want proficiency of shared topics while making initial contact.

## THEMATIC EVIDENCE
- Interest-based connecting appears in all groups (class project, sports clubs, gaming groups)
- Students repeatedly ask for interest visibility feature (hobbies, music, major, clubs)
- Group event success stories all involve shared activity
- App requirements consistently include interest-based discovery

## EVIDENCE FROM ALL STUDENTS BY SOURCE
- **Kai #1**: "find at least one shared interest ... otherwise it can be awkward."
- **Kai #2**: "same classes and similar interests, like sport." (netball)
- **Kai #3**: "running club helped me find like-minded people." (activity-based bonding)
- **Anindiyo #1**: "an app that could show you people with same interests." (explicit request)
- **Anindiyo #2**: "maybe their hobbies or interests... also maybe language." (multidimensional shared interest)
- **Serafina #1**: "talk about favourite things, passion; it makes positive vibe." (preferences matter)
- **Shenal #2 (Nadeesha)**: "everyone is in class to listen, club event easier because same reason." (purpose-driven interest)
- **Anya (Haruka)**: "I joined Japanese community events; they are easier because people already share the same culture and classes." (community + shared interest)
- **Anya (Lachlan)**: "I saw someone in a Brooklyn Nine-Nine T-shirt and used that as a reason to approach." (interest signal)
- **Anya (Sora)**: "Debate and dance clubs helped me meet people with shared activities." (interest-based club success)
- **Anya (Thomas)**: "Clubs and third places with like-minded people are the easiest places to start." (third-place shared interest)

## DECISION
**ACCEPTED**. Interest bridging is strong evidence and should be primary design principle.

- Add “shared activity” badges (same class/project/club) in discovery.
