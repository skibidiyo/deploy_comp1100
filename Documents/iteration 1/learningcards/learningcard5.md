#  Learning Card 5: App Features (Test Card 5 hypothesis)

## ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS
Students prefer a friend-finding app that includes interest-based matching and low-pressure interaction features (e.g., group chats or browsing profiles) over direct 1-to-1 matching.

## WHAT WE LEARNED FROM ALL INTERVIEWS
Strongly confirmed. Students consistently prefer apps that reduce pressure through indirect interaction (e.g., group settings, passive engagement) and rely on shared interests to initiate conversation. Direct 1-to-1 matching is perceived as more intense and higher pressure.

## THEMATIC EVIDENCE
Interest-based matching reduces awkwardness by providing immediate conversation starters
Low-pressure interaction (group chats, passive sharing, browsing) increases comfort and likelihood of engagement
Direct messaging feels high-stakes and requires effort to “carry” the conversation
Students prefer gradual interaction rather than immediate commitment
Apps are seen as safer than real-life interaction due to reduced fear of visible rejection
Existing platforms (e.g., Instagram) are seen as too superficial or not designed for meaningful connection

## DECISION
**ACCEPTED (strongly)**: Clear preference for interest-based matching and low-pressure interaction. Students actively avoid high-pressure, direct 1-to-1 engagement unless context is already established.

## NEXT STEP
Prioritize interest-based matching (hobbies, shows, activities) as the core feature
Implement low-pressure interaction options (group chats, prompts, passive content sharing)
Introduce conversation starters (games, shared prompts, activity-based interactions)
Avoid forcing immediate direct messaging; allow gradual engagement pathways
Design features that allow users to exit or disengage without social pressure
