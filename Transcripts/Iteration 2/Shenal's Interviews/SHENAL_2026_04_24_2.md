Interviewer:
Hello, do I have your consent for a short interview today?

Participant:
For what?

Interviewer:
For a short interview—a 5-minute interview.

Participant:
Oh, okay.

Interviewer:
So basically, I’m going to ask you around 10 questions, and they’ll be related to an app my team and I are developing. It’s about making new friends at uni and finding new people. All good?

Participant:
Yeah, that’s great.

Interviewer:
Okay, first question: have you heard or used apps like Tinder or Bumble before?

Participant:
Yeah, absolutely.

Interviewer:
How do you feel about an app like that, but instead of dating, it’s for making friends and new connections?

Participant:
Actually, I think that’s pretty good. You basically swipe, but instead of dating, you make connections. I get it. Bumble actually has a BFF mode, which is focused on finding friends. So I think something like that is very important, especially for new students.

Interviewer:
How important is it to you to know someone’s interests or hobbies before deciding to connect with them?

Participant:
I think that’s the most important thing. When you interact with someone, the first thing you talk about is similar interests. For example, if you both like music or know about a certain movie, you already have something to talk about and build from.

Interviewer:
Do their interests need to be very specific and closely matched to yours, or are general similarities enough?

Participant:
It’s better if you have the same exact interests, but similar ones are also okay. For example, if I love music and the other person loves art, I’d still be willing to learn about their interests.

Interviewer:
So you’d be open to learning new things just to be friends?

Participant:
Yeah, definitely.

Interviewer:
What kind of things on a profile would make you feel comfortable enough to start a conversation? What would you like to see—age, background, etc.?

Participant:
Besides the photo—because that’s obvious—I’d say hobbies and other details like age, gender, and what they do at uni.

Interviewer:
What group size is most comfortable for you when meeting new people?

Participant:
One-on-one is fine, but I think a group of three to four people is ideal.

Interviewer:
Do you find it helpful when there’s something to talk about already, like a shared topic or prompt?

Participant:
What do you mean?

Interviewer:
For example, before meeting in person, the app could give you a shared topic or prompt to start the conversation. Otherwise, you’d have to start from scratch.

Participant:
Yeah, definitely. Using chat in the app is the first step. You say hi, ask how they’re doing, learn a bit about them, and then maybe plan a hangout later.

Interviewer:
Would you like to know about nearby or upcoming uni events related to your interests to help meet new people?

Participant:
Absolutely.

Interviewer:
Can you elaborate on how that would help you?

Participant:
Events are usually based on clubs and societies. For example, if you join a football club and you like football, you’ll meet other people who like it too. Then you can talk about shared interests and connect more easily.

Interviewer:
Final question: are there any downsides to apps you already use, like Instagram or Facebook, that we could improve on?

Participant:
Those apps are good, but it would be better to have something specifically for uni students—like an app just for UQ students or people nearby. That would make it more relevant.

Interviewer:
Alright, thank you. That’s all the questions I have. Have a good day.
