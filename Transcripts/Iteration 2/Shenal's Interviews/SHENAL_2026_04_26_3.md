Interviewer:
Hello, do I have your consent for a short interview?

Participant:
Yeah, sure—you have my consent.

Interviewer:
So basically, I’m going to ask you 8–10 questions about a new app my team and I are developing for a project. I’ll start with the first question.

Participant:
Yeah, go ahead.

Interviewer:
Have you heard or used any apps like Tinder or Bumble?

Participant:
No, not that I recall. I’ve never used Tinder or Bumble.

Interviewer:
Okay, but you understand how they work, right?

Participant:
Yeah, I know the concept.

Interviewer:
How would you feel about an app like that where you swipe on people, but instead of dating it’s for making new friends?

Participant:
That’s a great idea, but I feel like it might be hard to get people on it. It might not be very diverse in terms of users. Some people are still unsure about apps like Tinder—not everyone is comfortable with them. So that might affect it. But overall, it’s a good idea to swipe and make new friends.

Interviewer:
How important is it to you to know someone’s interests or hobbies before deciding to connect with them?

Participant:
Not that important. I’ve had classes where I just talk to random people. They might ask if a seat is free, and then we start talking. Usually it’s general things like their name and what they study. At uni, you can also just exchange Instagram and connect that way. I don’t think hobbies are a major factor—you just need to introduce yourself and build from there.

Interviewer:
What kind of information on a profile would make you feel comfortable enough to connect with someone?

Participant:
If they’re doing the same course as me or similar subjects, that helps. Also, shared interests—like if they’re into cricket or sports. Anything that links us would help.

Interviewer:
What group size feels most comfortable when meeting new people?

Participant:
Anything above five is too much. Three to four people is ideal.

Interviewer:
So you prefer that over one-on-one?

Participant:
Yeah, but one-on-one is also fine.

Interviewer:
Do you find it helpful when there’s something to talk about already, like a shared topic or prompt?

Participant:
Yeah, that’s way better than starting from scratch. Otherwise, you don’t know what the other person is interested in. A shared topic makes it easier.

Interviewer:
Would you be open to a fun activity or game to get to know someone?

Participant:
Yeah, for sure.

Interviewer:
What kind of activity would feel natural?

Participant:
I’ve made friends online before—even people whose faces I don’t know—through mutual friends. We usually play games together. Gaming is a really good way to meet people and make friends.

Interviewer:
How do you feel about getting reminders about nearby or upcoming campus events related to your interests?

Participant:
That’s a great idea. It helps you meet new people. But those events can be crowded, and I don’t always feel comfortable in big groups. Still, it’s a good chance to build confidence and meet others.

Interviewer:
Are there any features that would make you use this app regularly?

Participant:
Maybe a calling feature or messaging feature to stay in touch. Something like Snapchat, where people post daily updates, could also help you stay connected and see what others are doing.

Interviewer:
Final question: are there any downsides to apps like Instagram or Discord that we could improve on?

Participant:
I don’t think Instagram is lacking much—they already have strong features. But maybe a better way to discover events. Right now, you have to follow pages to find events. It would be better if clubs could post announcements in one place, like a shared space for all events.

Interviewer:
Alright, that’s all the questions I have for you today. Thank you very much.

Participant:
Thank you. Have a good day.

Interviewer:
Thanks.
